# M4c1 — Verified masks and source-only depth feasibility

2026-10-03. One real **upper-character** cutout passes current geometric coverage/text guards; this is not broad chapter depth or a perceived 3D quality score. Candidate assets remain unpublished until reader integration in M4c2.

## Built / verified

Pinned official SAM 2.1 small adapter, reproducible D-only downloads/dependencies, existing sequential stage scheduler, page/geometry/revision caches, original-panel RGB prompts and binary PNG alpha masks. Source/checkpoint/runtime/API/license verification is in `pipeline/layers/DEPENDENCIES.md`. No art RGB is written into layer assets: PNG RGB is white and **alpha only** selects original page texture pixels. No inpainting or generated fill. The convenience checkpoint loader is replaced with verified bytes plus Torch's weights-only/strict-state loading.

Thirty raw masks were produced over five real pages (10/4/5/4/7). Visual mask review found printed text, stray background and panel-boundary contamination in some outputs. Raw scores are uncalibrated; they are never reported as semantic accuracy. Segmentation is separate from the CPU proof stage so later envelope tuning reuses every model result.

The proof constructs a union of conservative inverse source-pixel-cell rectangles over the entire permitted scale range, with a two-pixel bilinear guard. Inverse coordinates move toward an in-crop anchor for all scale ≥1 with zero translation. The union encloses every intermediate read, establishing continuous old-subject coverage rather than relying solely on sampled frames. Added alpha selects nearby **original RGB**, requiring a clean original-paper boundary. Original ink encountered inside the envelope is included in the covered subject; failure to converge is a rejection. Swept opaque-cell corner extrema at pose knots conservatively guard the panel and every text box. Output uses the already approved v2 safety method/hash fields; contract unchanged.

Full-body masks generally lack the clean surrounding pixels/space needed for these restrained poses. A bounded upper-character fallback keeps its lower attachment fixed. On **page 5, panel_000, char_001** it passes: source crop `[778,300,937,434]`, anchor `[.5471698113,1]`, scale 1→1.035→1.01, zero translation. It is the back-facing blond character's upper section; torso remains original and flat. Subject **14,022 pixels**, original-paper selection **2,692 pixels**, minimum selected-paper RGB **245**, zero uncovered pixels over 101 scale audits. Preparation views at scale 1/1.0175/1.035 were inspected at original-art resolution and as panel previews; no obvious seam, double outline or text change was seen in those views. This does not establish subjective depth quality or perfect segmentation of every character pixel.

The other **29 masks are rejected**, mainly no clean original-paper boundary, subject spanning panel height, text intersection or panel-crossing poses. No all-panel rejection is counted as successful depth. Full-character and wider panel coverage remain an explicit limitation; acceptance here is the one demonstrated eligible source region.

## Acceptance / risks

- [x] Verified/pinned segmentation adapter demonstrated on real pages and measured within 6 GB alone.
- [x] One eligible real upper-character region shown with source-only pixels; no generated background.
- [x] Continuous conservative coverage plus dense alpha audit; fixed text/panel guards and source/mask/transform hashes.
- [x] Unsafe/uncertain masks rejected with reasons; missing/tampered assets invalidate caches.
- [ ] Broad convincing full-character depth, dense actual-reader seam audit, actual-phone rendering and ≥4/5 depth comfort await M4c2/refinement and user evidence.

The white-paper condition is deliberately restrictive. Geometric coverage proves concealment of the prepared subject, not perfect model identification of all real character pixels. SAM output quality varies across these unrelated art styles. This delivery provides one safe preparation example, not a promise that every chapter has usable cutouts. The upper attachment and wider original-character boundaries need reader-scale review before production use.
